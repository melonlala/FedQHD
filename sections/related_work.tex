\section{Related Work}
 \label{sec:related_work}

\paragraph{Federated Reinforcement Learning with Shared Parameterizations.}
The dominant line of work in federated RL extends FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017communication} to the Q-learning or actor-critic setting by periodically averaging the parameters of identically structured neural networks across clients.
\cite{jin2022federated} propose Fed-DQN, the direct application of parameter averaging to deep Q-networks, and study its convergence under environment heterogeneity.
Because all clients share the same network architecture, the server can form a global model by a weighted mean of weight tensors, and each client resumes local training from this shared initialization.
This parameter-space paradigm is simple and communication-efficient, but it fundamentally requires \emph{identical architectures}: averaging weight matrices of different shapes is algebraically undefined, so the approach cannot be applied when clients hold encoders of different dimensionality or type.

\paragraph{Distillation-Based Federated RL for Heterogeneous Agents.}
To accommodate structural heterogeneity, a parallel line of work abandons parameter-space averaging in favour of knowledge transfer in \emph{policy} or \emph{Q-value} space.
\cite{rusu2016policydistillation} introduce policy distillation for single-agent compression: a large teacher network generates soft Q-value targets
$T(s) = \operatorname{softmax}(Q_{\text{teacher}}(s)/\tau)$,
and a student of arbitrary architecture is trained to minimise the KL divergence
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}} = \mathrm{KL}\!\left(T(s)\;\big\|\;\operatorname{softmax}(Q_{\text{student}}(s)/\tau)\right)$
over a set of distillation states.
Because the loss operates on Q-value outputs rather than weights, the teacher and student may have entirely different network widths or depths.

\cite{teh2017distral} extend this idea to the multi-task setting with \emph{Distral}: each worker $i$ maintains a task-specific policy $\pi_i$ and a shared distilled policy $\pi_0$ that captures common behaviour across tasks.
Workers are regularised to stay close to $\pi_0$ via a KL penalty, and $\pi_0$ is updated as the softmax of the arithmetic mean of workers' Q-values---equivalent to a function-space average.
Distral's key insight is that aggregating in Q-value space decouples knowledge sharing from parameter structure, enabling heterogeneous worker architectures.

\cite{shen2023fedhpd} combine these ideas in FedHPD, a federated heterogeneous policy distillation framework.
Clients with different DQN architectures evaluate their Q-functions on a shared distillation dataset; the server computes a teacher policy as the softmax of the mean Q-values; and each client performs several gradient steps to minimise the KL loss against this teacher.
The approach is architecture-agnostic, but federation requires \emph{iterative distillation steps} per round, incurring repeated forward and backward passes on each client that scale with the distillation dataset size and the number of gradient steps.

\paragraph{Black-Box and Query-Based Federated Q-Learning.}

\paragraph{Heterogeneous Environments and Tasks in Federated RL.}
Several works study heterogeneity in the \emph{reward or transition dynamics} rather than in the model structure.
\cite{jin2022federated} propose FRL-EH to handle clients with differing environment dynamics under a shared architecture.
\cite{hwang2025fed} extend this by introducing a regularisation term in the local update that penalises deviation from the global model, improving stability under non-i.i.d.\ environment distributions.
\cite{ye2023fedfm} propose anchor-based feature matching across clients with diverse observation spaces.
These methods still presuppose a shared parameter space and cannot handle clients whose Q-functions live in feature spaces of different dimensionality.

\paragraph{Proposed FedQHD.}
FedQHD addresses a strictly more general form of heterogeneity than any of the above.
Where parameter-averaging methods~\cite{jin2022federated} require identical architectures and distillation-based methods~\cite{shen2023fedhpd,teh2017distral} require iterative gradient steps, FedQHD targets \emph{structural heterogeneity} in which clients use different HDC encoders $\Phi_i$ with client-specific dimensions $D_i$, rendering parameter-space synchronisation algebraically undefined and iterative distillation expensive.

Like distillation-based approaches, FedQHD aggregates in \emph{function space}: clients evaluate their Q-functions on a shared anchor set $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and the server forms a global teacher $Q^{\mathrm{glob}}_{\mathrm{ref}}$ as a weighted mean of the resulting Q-value matrices.
Unlike distillation, however, the back-projection from the global teacher into each client's private representation is a \emph{one-shot closed-form ridge regression}
$W^{\mathrm{glob}}_i = \left(X_i^\mathrm{H} X_i + \lambda I\right)^{-1} X_i^\mathrm{H} Q^{\mathrm{glob}}_{\mathrm{ref}}$,
requiring no iterative gradient steps on the client.
This closed form is tractable precisely because QHD Q-functions are linear in their parameters.
Furthermore, FedQHD admits a homogeneous limit: when all clients share the same encoder, anchor-based aggregation reduces \emph{exactly} to FedAvg parameter averaging, providing a principled bridge between the classical and heterogeneous federation regimes.
