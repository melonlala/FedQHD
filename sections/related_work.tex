\section{Related Work}
 \label{sec:related_work}

\paragraph{Federated Reinforcement Learning with Shared Parameterizations.}
The dominant line of work in federated RL extends FedAvg~\citep{mcmahan2017communication} to the Q-learning or actor-critic setting by periodically averaging the parameters of identically structured neural networks across clients.
\citet{jin2022federated} propose Fed-DQN, the direct application of parameter averaging to deep Q-networks, and study its convergence under environment heterogeneity.
Because all clients share the same network architecture, the server can form a global model by a weighted mean of weight tensors, and each client resumes local training from this shared initialization.
This parameter-space paradigm is simple and communication-efficient, but it fundamentally requires \emph{identical architectures}: averaging weight matrices of different shapes is algebraically undefined, so the approach cannot be applied when clients hold encoders of different dimensionality or type.

\paragraph{Distillation-Based Federated RL for Heterogeneous Agents.}
To accommodate structural heterogeneity, a parallel line of work abandons parameter-space averaging in favour of knowledge transfer in \emph{policy} or \emph{Q-value} space.
\citet{rusu2016policydistillation} introduce policy distillation for single-agent compression: a large teacher network generates soft Q-value targets
$T(s) = \operatorname{softmax}(Q_{\text{teacher}}(s)/\tau)$,
and a student of arbitrary architecture is trained to minimise the KL divergence
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}} = \mathrm{KL}\!\left(T(s)\;\big\|\;\operatorname{softmax}(Q_{\text{student}}(s)/\tau)\right)$
over a set of distillation states.
Because the loss operates on Q-value outputs rather than weights, the teacher and student may have entirely different network widths or depths.

\citet{teh2017distral} extend this idea to the multi-task setting with \emph{Distral}: each worker $i$ maintains a task-specific policy $\pi_i$ and a shared distilled policy $\pi_0$ that captures common behaviour across tasks.
Workers are regularised to stay close to $\pi_0$ via a KL penalty, and $\pi_0$ is updated as the softmax of the arithmetic mean of workers' Q-values---equivalent to a function-space average.
Distral's key insight is that aggregating in Q-value space decouples knowledge sharing from parameter structure, enabling heterogeneous worker architectures.

\citet{shen2023fedhpd} combine these ideas in FedHPD, a federated heterogeneous policy distillation framework.
Clients with different DQN architectures evaluate their Q-functions on a shared distillation dataset; the server computes a teacher policy as the softmax of the mean Q-values; and each client performs several gradient steps to minimise the KL loss against this teacher.
The approach is architecture-agnostic, but federation requires \emph{iterative distillation steps} per round, incurring repeated forward and backward passes on each client that scale with the distillation dataset size and the number of gradient steps.

\paragraph{Black-Box and Query-Based Federated Q-Learning.}
\citet{fan2023fedhql} propose FedHQL, a federated heterogeneous Q-learning framework in which clients with different Q-network architectures evaluate their Q-functions on a shared query set and the server aggregates the resulting Q-value matrices.
Like FedQHD, FedHQL operates in function space and requires no assumptions on architecture compatibility.
However, FedHQL employs a model-free server estimator that requires multiple query rounds and does not admit a closed-form update; furthermore, it lacks a theoretical characterization of the information loss induced by client heterogeneity.
FedQHD achieves what FedHQL does in one aggregation round via ridge regression, and our federation gap bound directly quantifies the heterogeneity cost as a function of encoder dimension and anchor count.

\paragraph{Heterogeneous Environments and Tasks in Federated RL.}
Several works study heterogeneity in the \emph{reward or transition dynamics} rather than in the model structure.
\citet{jin2022federated} propose FRL-EH to handle clients with differing environment dynamics under a shared architecture.
\citet{hwang2025fed} extend this by introducing a regularisation term in the local update that penalises deviation from the global model, improving stability under non-i.i.d.\ environment distributions.
\citet{ye2023fedfm} propose anchor-based feature matching across clients with diverse observation spaces.
These methods still presuppose a shared parameter space and cannot handle clients whose Q-functions live in feature spaces of different dimensionality.

\paragraph{HDC for Reinforcement Learning.}
\citet{ni2022qhd} introduced QHD, the single-agent HDC Q-learning algorithm that forms the computational backbone of FedQHD. QHD encodes continuous states as hypervectors and maintains linear Q-function parameters updated by semi-gradient TD, achieving competitive performance on classic control tasks with significantly lower memory and training time than deep Q-networks. FedQHD inherits QHD's linear-in-parameters structure and extends it to the multi-agent federated setting, where this linearity enables provably exact function-space aggregation.
HDC has also been applied to other RL problems and broader machine learning tasks; the key property exploited here—that random feature methods yield finite-dimensional RKHS approximations with $O(D^{-1/2})$ uniform error \citep{rahimi2007random,rudi2017generalization}—is well established in the kernel learning literature \citep{bach2015equivalence}.

\paragraph{Linear Function Approximation in Federated RL Theory.}
The closest theoretical precursors to our analysis are results on federated temporal-difference learning with linear function approximation \citep{wang2024federated,khodadadian2022federated}.
These works study convergence rates for federated TD under shared feature representations, establishing linear speedup in the number of clients.
\citet{tsitsiklis1997analysis} and \citet{bhandari2018finite} provide the foundational single-agent convergence results for linear TD from which federated extensions depart.
FedQHD differs from this line of work in two respects: (i)~it targets the heterogeneous encoder regime where clients use different feature maps, and (ii)~it provides a static federation gap bound rather than a convergence rate, characterizing the quality of the aggregated Q-function in terms of geometric quantities (principal angles, Gram matrix eigenvalues) rather than stochastic approximation error terms.

\paragraph{Proposed FedQHD.}
FedQHD addresses a strictly more general form of heterogeneity than any of the above.
Where parameter-averaging methods~\cite{jin2022federated} require identical architectures and distillation-based methods~\cite{shen2023fedhpd,teh2017distral} require iterative gradient steps, FedQHD targets \emph{structural heterogeneity} in which clients use different HDC encoders $\Phi_i$ with client-specific dimensions $D_i$, rendering parameter-space synchronisation algebraically undefined and iterative distillation expensive.

Like distillation-based approaches, FedQHD aggregates in \emph{function space}: clients evaluate their Q-functions on a shared anchor set $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and the server forms a global teacher $Q^{\mathrm{glob}}_{\mathrm{ref}}$ as a weighted mean of the resulting Q-value matrices.
Unlike distillation, however, the back-projection from the global teacher into each client's private representation is a \emph{one-shot closed-form ridge regression}
$W^{\mathrm{glob}}_i = \left(X_i^\mathrm{H} X_i + \lambda I\right)^{-1} X_i^\mathrm{H} Q^{\mathrm{glob}}_{\mathrm{ref}}$,
requiring no iterative gradient steps on the client.
This closed form is tractable precisely because QHD Q-functions are linear in their parameters.
Furthermore, FedQHD admits a homogeneous limit: when all clients share the same encoder, anchor-based aggregation reduces \emph{exactly} to FedAvg parameter averaging, providing a principled bridge between the classical and heterogeneous federation regimes.
