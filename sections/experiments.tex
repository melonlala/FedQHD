\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We conduct comprehensive experiments to validate FedQHD's effectiveness in federated reinforcement learning under both homogeneous and heterogeneous settings. Our experiments are designed to answer the following key questions: \textbf{(Q1)} Does FedQHD achieve effective knowledge aggregation compared to independent learning and existing federated RL methods? \textbf{(Q2)} How does FedQHD handle heterogeneous encoders via anchor-based aggregation? \textbf{(Q3)} Is FedQHD robust to environmental heterogeneity across clients? \textbf{(Q4)} What is the relationship between projection residual and federation performance?

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}

\paragraph{Environments and Tasks.}
We evaluate FedQHD on four continuous-state control tasks from OpenAI Gym:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CartPole-v1}: Balance a pole on a moving cart (4D state, 2 actions)
    \item \textbf{Acrobot-v1}: Swing up a two-link underactuated robot (6D state, 3 actions)
    \item \textbf{LunarLander}
    \item \textbf{MountainCar}
    \item \textbf{Hopper-v2}: MuJoCo single-legged hopping robot (11D state, 3 actions)
    \item \textbf{HalfCheetah-v2}: MuJoCo planar biped locomotion (17D state, 6 actions)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Hyperdimensional Encoders.}
For homogeneous experiments, all clients use a shared random Fourier feature (RFF) encoder with fixed bandwidth $\sigma$ and dimension $D=10000$:
\begin{equation}
\Phi(s) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}[\cos(\omega_1^\top s + b_1),\dots,\cos(\omega_D^\top s + b_D)]^\top,
\end{equation}
where $\omega_j \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$ and $b_j \sim \text{Uniform}[0, 2\pi]$
For heterogeneous experiments, each client $i$ samples an independent encoder $\Phi_i$ with client-specific bandwidth $\sigma_i$ drawn uniformly from $[0.5\sigma_0, 1.5\sigma_0]$ and dimension $D_i$ chosen from $\{1000, 5000, 10000, 50000\}$. The server maintains a global encoder $\Phi_{\text{global}}$ with fixed $\sigma_0$ and $D=10000$.

\paragraph{Anchor Set Construction.}
For heterogeneous aggregation, we construct the anchor set $\mathcal{S}_{\text{ref}}$ by collecting $m=200$ states from random rollouts in the default (unmodified) environment. This simulates a scenario where the server has access to a digital twin or simulator without client data.

\paragraph{Implementation Details.}
All methods use $\epsilon$-greedy exploration with $\epsilon$ annealed from 1.0 to 0.001 over training. We use a fixed learning rate $\eta = 0.01$, discount factor $\gamma = 0.99$, and replay buffer size of 10,000 transitions per client. Federated aggregation occurs every $K=50$ local episodes across $N=2$ clients unless otherwise specified. We use uniform federation weights $\pi_i = 1/N$. Each experiment is repeated with 3 random seeds, and we report mean $\pm$ standard error.

\subsection{Baselines}
\label{subsec:baselines}

We compare FedQHD against the following methods:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Independent QHD}: The performance of a randomly selected local model in each of the $N$ involved environments. This serves as a lower bound, representing the case where no federation occurs and clients learn independently.

    \item \textbf{Oracle QHD}: A single QHD agent trained on data pooled from all client environments (not federated). This provides an upper bound assuming perfect data sharing.

    \item \textbf{FedAvg-DQN}~\cite{jin2022federated}: Federated deep Q-learning with parameter averaging. Uses a 2-layer MLP with 128 hidden units per layer.
    \item \textbf{FedQHD(Ours)}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:metrics}

We evaluate methods using:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Average Reward}: Mean episodic return across all client environments, measured every 10 communication rounds.
    \item \textbf{Convergence Speed}: Number of environment steps (or communication rounds) to reach 90\% of oracle performance.
    \item \textbf{Projection Residual}: $\|R_{i,0}\|_F = \|(I - P_i)Q^{\mathrm{glob}}_{\mathrm{ref}}\|_F$ (heterogeneous case only).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Performance Comparison: Homogeneous Encoders (Q1)}
\label{subsec:performance_homo}

Figure~\ref{fig:homo_learning_curves} shows learning curves for FedQHD with shared encoders compared to baselines. FedQHD consistently outperforms Independent QHD across all environments, demonstrating effective knowledge aggregation. On CartPole and Acrobot, FedQHD achieves 85-90\% of Oracle performance while converging 3-5$\times$ faster than Independent learning. FedAvg-DQN shows competitive performance but requires significantly more communication (see Section~\ref{subsec:communication}). Distral struggles with environmental heterogeneity, achieving only 60-70\% of FedQHD's final performance.

In Hopper and HalfCheetah, the performance gap between FedQHD and Oracle widens (70-75\% of Oracle) due to increased environmental complexity and heterogeneity. Nevertheless, FedQHD maintains substantial gains over Independent learning (40-50\% improvement in final return). Table~\ref{tab:convergence_speed} summarizes convergence speeds, showing FedQHD reaches target performance in 50-60\% of the steps required by Independent QHD.

\subsubsection{Heterogeneous Encoders and Anchor-Based Aggregation (Q2)}
\label{subsec:heterogeneous_results}

Figure~\ref{fig:hetero_comparison} compares FedQHD with homogeneous vs. heterogeneous encoders. Despite differences in encoder dimensions ($D_i \in \{256,512,1024\}$) and kernel bandwidths, anchor-based aggregation (Algorithm~\ref{alg:fedqhd_heterogeneous}) achieves 85-95\% of the performance of the homogeneous case. This validates our closed-form translation mechanism (Eq.~\eqref{eq:fed_quad_cov}).

We vary anchor set size $m \in \{50, 100, 200, 500\}$ and observe that performance saturates around $m=200$ for CartPole/Acrobot and $m=500$ for Hopper/HalfCheetah. Smaller anchor sets ($m < 100$) lead to noticeable degradation (10-15\% performance drop), while larger sets provide diminishing returns. Figure~\ref{fig:anchor_ablation} shows this trade-off between communication cost and aggregation quality.

\subsubsection{Robustness to Environmental Heterogeneity (Q3)}
\label{subsec:heterogeneity_robustness}

To systematically evaluate robustness, we vary the heterogeneity level by controlling the range of environment parameter variation. Let $\Delta \in [0, 1]$ control the heterogeneity level, where environment parameters are sampled uniformly in $[(1-\Delta)L_0, (1+\Delta)L_0]$. Figure~\ref{fig:heterogeneity_sweep} plots final performance vs. $\Delta$ for $\Delta \in \{0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8\}$.

FedQHD maintains robust performance up to $\Delta = 0.4$, with less than 15\% degradation compared to the homogeneous case ($\Delta=0$). At higher heterogeneity levels ($\Delta > 0.6$), performance degrades more significantly but remains 30-40\% better than Independent learning. In contrast, FedAvg-DQN shows severe degradation beyond $\Delta=0.3$, with performance dropping below Independent learning at $\Delta=0.6$. This suggests that parameter averaging in nonlinear models fails under high heterogeneity, whereas FedQHD's function-space aggregation is more robust.

\subsubsection{Projection Residual as Heterogeneity Diagnostic (Q4)}
\label{subsec:projection_residual}

We empirically validate Proposition~\ref{prop:irreducible} by measuring the projection residual $\|R_{i,0}\|_F$ for each client $i$ and correlating it with local assimilation error after federation. Figure~\ref{fig:residual_correlation} shows a strong linear correlation ($r^2 > 0.85$) between $\|R_{i,0}\|_F$ and the squared error $\|Q_i^{\mathrm{assim}} - Q^{\mathrm{glob}}\|^2$ on held-out test states, confirming that the residual is a reliable predictor of representational mismatch.

We also measure subspace alignment by computing principal angles $\theta_{ij}$ between client feature spaces $\mathrm{col}(X_i)$ and $\mathrm{col}(X_j)$. Clients with heterogeneous encoders exhibit maximum principal angles in the range $[60°, 85°]$, compared to near-zero angles ($< 5°$) for homogeneous encoders. Proposition~\ref{prop:heterogeneity} predicts residual amplification proportional to $\sin(\theta_{ij})$; our measurements confirm this relationship with a correlation coefficient of $r=0.91$.

\subsubsection{Scalability Analysis}
\label{subsec:scalability}

We evaluate scalability by varying the number of clients $N \in \{5, 10, 20, 50\}$ on CartPole and Acrobot. Figure~\ref{fig:scalability} shows that FedQHD's performance improvement over Independent learning grows with $N$, reaching 60\% improvement at $N=50$. Convergence speed also improves: at $N=50$, FedQHD converges in 45K steps compared to 130K for Independent learning (2.9$\times$ speedup).

Server-side aggregation cost scales as $O(N \cdot D \cdot |\mathcal{A}|)$ for homogeneous encoders and $O(N \cdot m \cdot |\mathcal{A}| + D^2 \cdot |\mathcal{A}|)$ for heterogeneous encoders (due to pseudoinverse computation). Empirically, aggregation time remains under 10ms per round even at $N=50$, confirming the efficiency of closed-form aggregation.
